# Dataset curation: audit of our data and best practice (2026-11-09)

Tags: [P] primary source, [S] secondary, [L] measured on our data, [I] inference.
- **Audit scripts:** `research/scripts/curation_audit.py`, `research/scripts/channel_audit.py`; outputs in `research/{curation,channel}_audit.json`.
- **Sources:** one research pass, 2026-11-09. URLs are inline where they matter.
- **Training set audited:** what E9 trains on, i.e. E6's bundle plus v1 data for the 12 MyGO!!!!! / Ave Mujica characters, with the Soyo and Sakiko persona rows folded. That is 17,034 training clips: v1 game 8,544 (1,215 min), Our Notes 7,766 (543 min), v1 anime 617 (33 min), v1 mixed 107 (6 min).

## 1. What the audit found [L]

1. **The same character in two games is close to two different voices to the speaker encoder.** For the 11 merged characters, v1 and Our Notes clips are 94–100% separable by a linear classifier on speaker-encoder embeddings.
   - Centroid cosine between the two eras per character:

     | voice | cosine |
     |---|---|
     | Anon | 0.19 |
     | Soyo | 0.29 |
     | Sakiko | 0.41 |
     | Taki | 0.46 |
     | Tomori | 0.50 |
     | Rāna | 0.55 |
     | Uika | 0.58 |
     | Mutsumi | 0.63 |
     | Mortis | 0.64 |
     | Umiri | 0.70 |
     | Nyamu | 0.74 |

   - For comparison, Sakiko's two personas are 85–89% separable, and two different characters 99–100%.
   - **Part of it is the recording channel:**

     | | Our Notes | v1 |
     |---|---|---|
     | 99% spectral roll-off | 8.3 kHz | 6.5 kHz |
     | energy above 8 kHz | 1.2% | 0.4% |
     | noise floor below speech | 34.6 dB | 40 dB |

     Our Notes is brighter and a little noisier.
   - **The other part is the performance:** a different game, years apart.
   - This matches E8's listening: Anon and Umiri sounded clearly better in v1.
2. **v1's Girls Band Party voices have almost no short lines.** The source pack's clips are long, but whole lines: median 8.8 s, only 0.5% under 1.5 s, and none rejected by our build. So the 37 unchanged voices have never been trained on short lines or interjections. Our Notes covers only its own 28 characters (checked: 27 speakers in the extraction), so there is no local source of short lines for the 37.
3. **Transcripts are mostly sound.**
   - Best-of-two ASR CER is 0 for 75% of v1 game clips and 62% of Our Notes clips. Above 0.10: 1.5% of v1 game clips, 17% of Our Notes clips.
   - The Our Notes excess is drawn-out interjections (a short exclamation with a long run of wave dashes, or a hum trailing into an ellipsis), where ASR is meaningless and the script text is the right label.
   - Our gate already reads in kana space with a name dictionary and a lenient per-voice bar of 0.25 (ReazonSpeech uses 0.33 [P]).
4. **Speaker purity is reasonable.** With per-source centring:
   - 6% (v1) and 18% (Our Notes) of clips sit nearer another voice's centroid than their own. These are mostly the persona pairs and the crowded new young voices, where nearest-centroid is a weak test anyway.
   - **A conservative outlier rule flags 1.2–1.7% (244 clips),** spread evenly over expression classes. The rule: ≥ 2 s, robust z < −3.5 within the voice, *and* nearer another voice.
5. **Minor findings:**
   - Speaking-rate outliers: 0.1% of clips.
   - Same-text different-take rows: 192 (kept; takes carry variation).
   - No clipping seen: Our Notes peak p90 is 0.45.
   - The anime-sourced clips' dynamic range is comparable to the game audio.
   - We also found and fixed a mapping bug in our own audit script: Our Notes clips' vectors are indexed by the manifest's `vec` field directly, not through `on_vec.idx`.

## 2. What published pipelines do [P]

- **Quality filters:**
  - percentile cuts on DNSMOS/NISQA (Raon: dropping the worst 15% beat dropping none or 50%; TTS-1: worst 20%);
  - DNSMOS thresholds (Emilia > 3.0, FireRedTTS > 3.3).
  - Enhancement only for low-SNR audio: FireRedTTS found it hurts clean audio.
- **Transcript gates are lenient or agreement-based:** ReazonSpeech CER ≤ 0.33; CosyVoice 3 pairwise WER of 3 ASR systems < 15%; ASR confidence ≥ 0.7–0.8. The script text stays the label.
- **Speaker purity:**
  - drop clips far from the speaker centroid (FireRedTTS, WenetSpeech4TTS);
  - a speaker-change filter raised SIM 0.707 → 0.719 (2406.05699).
- **Domains:**
  - CosyVoice 2 uses speaker tags in multi-speaker fine-tuning.
  - ZeSTA's real/synthetic **domain label** restored speaker similarity (SECS 0.765 → 0.807; 0.815 with 3× oversampling of real data); oversampling alone was "limited and unstable".
  - JVS keeps whisper and falsetto as labelled subsets, not filtered out.
  - No published evidence on recording-era drift.
- **Short lines:** pipelines set a 1–3 s minimum and **join** adjacent same-speaker segments (Emilia, FireRedTTS, TTS-1 with 0.5–1.5 s gaps). Speaker embeddings are unreliable under 2 s (ERes2NetV2 EER 0.61% → 1.48%).
- **Japanese text:** Galgame/anime-whisper conventions collapse repeated symbols to ≤ 2 and keep 〜/ー/っ/！？/… IndexTTS's mixed-reading trick fixed name pronunciation (20% of characters as readings in 50% of samples).
- **Gaps:** no codec-LM punctuation ablation, and no validated near-duplicate threshold.

## 3. Recommendations, ranked for our data [I]

1. **Handle the two eras explicitly, not by blind pooling.** This is the user's decision for E9; see below.
2. **Prune the 244 conservative speaker outliers.** About 1.5%, evenly spread over expression classes. Literature-backed, low risk. In E9 by default.
3. **Keep the transcript gate as it is,** with interjections kept on their script text. It already matches best practice; tightening it would remove the expressive short lines we want.
4. **Short lines for the 37 v1 voices (later):**
   - No real source exists.
   - Options: voice-convert Our Notes interjections into those voices with the 15 E5 converters (VC augmentation, needs listening); or, at inference, best-of-N for short lines in the Space.
5. **Synthetic-data domain label (later):** tag pseudo and VC rows apart from real rows (ZeSTA). It needs a tag token; untested on Qwen3-TTS.
6. **Not worth doing now:**
   - speaking-rate filtering (0.1%);
   - denoising game audio (it can hurt);
   - DNSMOS cuts on game audio (UTMOS and DNSMOS penalise expressive speech);
   - punctuation re-writing (no evidence for codec-LMs).

## 4. The era decision for E9

| Option | What it means | Fits |
|---|---|---|
| **One voice per character, anchored** | Both eras' data under one id; the speaker vector built from the era the listener preferred in E8 (v1 for Anon and Umiri; mixed or neutral elsewhere) | The "merge" request; but the model still learns a blend, and which era comes out may depend on the text |
| **Two ids per character in one model** | e.g. `mygo_anon` (v1 era, the default) and `mygo_anon_ournotes`; one shared model trains on both | The audit (two voices to the encoder) and controllability; the merge benefit (shared training) is kept |
| **Plain merge** | Both eras under one id, the vector from both | Simplest; the most likely to blur |
